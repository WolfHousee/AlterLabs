A chatbot for the questions your customers ask most

The operational problem

Customers ask the same questions again and again: what you offer, how it works and what happens next. Every answer waits until someone on the team is free to type it.

AlterLabs designs a chatbot around the questions a business actually receives: which ones it answers on its own, which ones belong with a person, and what it records so the conversation is not lost.

What AlterLabs builds

A chatbot that greets the visitor, works out what they are asking for, answers from material you have approved, and passes anything beyond that to the right person.

What the chatbot is allowed to say is agreed with you before any customer sees it.

  • Question scope and conversation design
  • Answers drawn from material you approve
  • Visitor intent capture and routing
  • Handover to a person, with the conversation so far
  • Conversation summaries for the team
  • Enquiry details passed into the systems you already run
  • A fallback path when the chatbot is unsure
  • Review and adjustment after the first live conversations

Where this helps

A chatbot suits the questions that repeat: the same few topics, the same details to collect, the same handful of next steps.

Questions that need judgment, a quote or a commitment stay with a person, and the chatbot hands them over with the conversation attached.

Who this is best for

  • Businesses answering the same questions every day
  • Teams whose enquiries arrive while everyone is busy
  • Services that collect the same details before a booking
  • Owners who want to see what customers actually ask
  • Teams that want a person to make every commitment

Clear scope before implementation

Chatbot work is quoted against a written scope: which questions the chatbot answers, what it may say, where it hands over and which systems it needs to reach. The first release is kept narrow so real conversations can be reviewed before the scope grows.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the answers come from?

From material you approve: how you describe what you offer, how you work and what you do not do. The chatbot is not asked to go beyond it.

What happens when the chatbot cannot help?

The conversation is handed to a person, with what the customer has already written passed along, so nobody has to start again.

Will customers know they are talking to a chatbot?

That is your decision, and the greeting is written with you before anything goes live.

Does this replace the team?

No. It takes the questions that repeat so the team can spend its time on the conversations that need a person.
